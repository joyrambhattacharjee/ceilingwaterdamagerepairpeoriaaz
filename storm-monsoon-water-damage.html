During A Storm

What to do when your ceiling leaks

If a ceiling starts leaking during a monsoon, act to limit the damage: move valuables and furniture out from under the leak, place a bucket to catch water, and call us. If the ceiling is bulging with trapped water, keep clear and let our team relieve it safely rather than risking a sudden collapse from below. The faster water is removed after a storm, the less chance of mold and the more of your ceiling we can save rather than replace.

Will insurance cover monsoon ceiling damage?

Sudden storm damage is often covered, unlike long-term wear. We document the loss thoroughly so you can file with confidence; confirm specifics with your insurer.

Why are tile roofs prone to monsoon leaks?

Wind-driven rain can force water under cracked or lifted tiles and past worn underlayment, and debris can block drainage so water pools and finds a way in.

Can storm water cause mold?

Yes — if saturated ceilings and attics are not dried quickly, mold can develop within a day or two. Fast drying is the best prevention.

Do you handle the roof and the ceiling?

We trace and coordinate the roof-leak source and restore the interior ceiling, so the whole problem is solved rather than just the visible damage.

Is it worth calling for a small storm stain?

Yes. A small stain after a storm often means water entered the attic, and catching it early keeps the repair small and the mold risk low.

Signs You Need It

Spotting monsoon ceiling damage

After a storm, check ceilings for new stains, damp patches, bubbling paint or a sagging area — especially near the roofline and around fixtures. A stain that appears or grows after a monsoon almost always means water entered through the roof. Should I wait to see if the ceiling dries after a storm?

Can monsoon damage be prevented?

Is storm ceiling damage always from the roof?
